## CLEAR and COSTAR prompt frameworks

CLEAR is a peer-reviewed framework introduced by Leo S. Lo. Its five principles are **Concise**, **Logical**, **Explicit**, **Adaptive**, and **Reflective**. The available institutional record describes it as a framework for improving interactions with language models and notes technical prompt factors such as tokens, temperature, and top-p.

COSTAR is a practitioner framework presented as a structural template: **Context**, **Objective**, **Style**, **Tone**, **Audience**, and **Response**. Its linked source is a secondary Medium article, so use it as a practical checklist rather than an authoritative or empirically established standard.

## Use as design prompts, not rigid templates

- Use CLEAR to review whether an instruction is brief enough, logically ordered, explicit about outcome, adaptable to feedback, and revised reflectively.
- Use COSTAR when a blank page needs a first-pass structure for task context, objective, communication, audience, and response shape.
- Do not force every section into every skill. Keep only the fields that change execution quality or make later evaluation clearer.
- Prefer the skill specification's approved headings when they are the local convention; map framework ideas into that structure rather than creating competing schemas.
