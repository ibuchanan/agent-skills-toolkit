# Write a Research Project Plan

A research plan translates an abstract topic into a concrete, researchable
project. It clarifies goals, timeframe, methodology, resources, and expected
deliverables, and it serves as a guideline for carrying out and reporting the
work.

## Key takeaways

- A research plan helps clarify project goals, timeframe, methodology, and resources needed.
- State the rationale, overall aim, and specific aims, and show how they relate.
- Distinguish outcomes (new knowledge) from outputs (tangible products).
- Check that the final plan is coherent: aims → methods → outcomes/outputs.
- A detailed plan can be condensed into a 1–2 page summary for collaborators, partners, or investors.

## Plan structure

1. **Rationale for the project**
   - Why this project? What gap in knowledge does it address?
   - Why does it matter now? Is it tied to a real-world problem?
   - A clear rationale helps formulate aims and hypotheses.

2. **Aims, objectives, research questions, and/or hypotheses**
   - One overarching aim that captures the whole project.
   - Two to four specific aims that together address the overarching aim.
   - Show how specific aims interconnect and whether any depend on others.

3. **Methodology**
   - What approach will answer the aims?
   - What additional support, expertise, or resources are needed?
   - What is a realistic timeframe? Be honest; over-ambition forces later cuts.

4. **Support required**
   - Collaborators, research assistants, end-user partners, equipment, infrastructure, funding.
   - Account for lead time to organise people, access, and approvals.

5. **Outcomes and outputs**
   - Outcomes: findings, results, new knowledge.
   - Outputs: publications, tools, datasets, websites, patents, conference papers.

6. **Coherence check**
   - The methods must address the aims.
   - The outcomes/outputs must embody the achievement of those aims.
   - Revisit and revise the plan as the project evolves.

## Implications for the skill

A `research-references` research plan should be much lighter than a full
academic project plan, but it should preserve the same discipline:

- Start from the user’s question and why it matters to the skill/spec.
- Define specific aims (e.g., find authoritative source X, compare primary vs. secondary sources, verify an AI-surfaced citation).
- Choose methods (search strategy, authority criteria, verification steps).
- Identify support (e.g., whether to delegate fetching to another skill).
- Define outputs (a list of evaluated sources, structured reference files).
- Check coherence before executing.
